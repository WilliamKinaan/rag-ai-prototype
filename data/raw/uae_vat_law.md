# UAE VAT Law — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from Federal Decree-Law No. 8 of 2017 on Value Added Tax (in
force 1 Jan 2018) and its executive regulation (Cabinet Decision No. 52
of 2017), as published by the Federal Tax Authority (tax.gov.ae)._

The UAE introduced VAT at a standard rate of 5% on most supplies of
goods and services and on imports — a comparatively low rate next to
many VAT/GST systems elsewhere, and one of the first broad-based
federal taxes the UAE ever levied, since the country had no general
sales or income tax before this. Businesses that exceed a mandatory
registration threshold of taxable supplies and imports must register
with the Federal Tax Authority (FTA) and begin charging, collecting,
and remitting VAT on their sales, filing periodic returns.

Certain categories are taxed differently rather than at the standard
5%: some supplies are zero-rated (taxed at 0%, but the seller can still
reclaim VAT paid on related costs) — including exports outside the UAE
and specific healthcare and education services — while others are fully
exempt (no VAT charged, and input VAT generally cannot be reclaimed),
such as many bare land and local passenger transport transactions. This
zero-rated/exempt distinction is a common source of confusion, since
both look like "no VAT charged" to a customer but have very different
consequences for the seller's own VAT recovery.

Non-compliance — late registration, late filing, or under-reporting —
is enforced through administrative penalties set by the FTA rather than
criminal prosecution in the ordinary case, though deliberate tax evasion
can escalate beyond administrative fines.

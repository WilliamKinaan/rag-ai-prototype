# UAE Bankruptcy and Financial Restructuring Law — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from Federal Decree-Law No. 51 of 2023 on Financial
Restructuring and Bankruptcy (in force 1 May 2024, replacing Federal
Decree-Law No. 9 of 2016), as summarized on the UAE Government's
official platform (u.ae)._

Federal Decree-Law No. 51 of 2023 replaced the UAE's earlier 2016
bankruptcy law with a framework more explicitly oriented toward helping
distressed but viable companies restructure and avoid liquidation,
rather than defaulting quickly to winding the company up. It offers
several formal routes short of full bankruptcy — including protective
composition procedures, which give a struggling company court-supervised
breathing room to negotiate a settlement with creditors while continuing
to operate, rather than facing immediate creditor enforcement action.

A notable design goal of the law is preserving the value of the
company's business and jobs during restructuring rather than only
protecting creditors: the law explicitly balances the interests of
creditors, employees, and other stakeholders, and encourages negotiated,
amicable settlements over contested liquidation proceedings where
possible. Only once restructuring genuinely isn't viable does the
process move to formal bankruptcy and liquidation, administered under
court supervision with an appointed trustee overseeing the process.

The law applies onshore across the UAE; the DIFC and ADGM financial free
zones each maintain their own separate insolvency regimes rather than
falling under this federal law, consistent with how those two free
zones handle most other areas of commercial law.

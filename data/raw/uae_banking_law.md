# UAE Banking and Central Bank Regulation — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Originally enacted as Federal Decree-Law No. 14 of 2018 Concerning the
Central Bank and the Regulation of Financial Institutions and
Activities; now consolidated into Federal Decree-Law No. 6 of 2025 —
see the companion doc on UAE insurance regulation for the same law's
insurance side. Sourced from the Central Bank of the UAE (CBUAE) Rulebook and
reputable legal-industry summaries._

The Central Bank's governing law replaced a much older 1980 framework —
the first substantive update to central-bank regulation since the
UAE's original 1973 law — reorganizing how banks and other licensed
financial institutions are supervised. The Central Bank's core mandates
under this framework are protecting the stability of the UAE's financial
system, prudently managing the country's foreign reserves, and
maintaining the stability of the national currency, rather than acting
as a general commercial regulator for every kind of business.

A structural point that surprises people used to a single national
regulator: this law explicitly does not apply inside the DIFC or ADGM.
Those two financial free zones regulate their own banks and financial
institutions independently, through their own regulators (the DFSA in
the DIFC, the FSRA in ADGM) — so "licensed by the UAE Central Bank" and
"licensed to operate a financial business in the UAE" are not the same
statement once a free zone is involved.

Since 2025, this Central Bank framework and the UAE's insurance
regulation (see the companion doc) sit under the same consolidated
decree-law, reflecting a broader trend of folding UAE financial
regulation under one umbrella body rather than maintaining separate
standalone laws per financial sector.

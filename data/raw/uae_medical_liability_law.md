# UAE Medical Liability Law — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from Federal Decree-Law No. 4 of 2016 Concerning Medical
Liability and its executive regulation (Cabinet Resolution No. 40 of
2019), Ministry of Health and Prevention (MOHAP) official materials._

Federal Decree-Law No. 4 of 2016 defines what counts as a medical error
and sets out the obligations and prohibitions that apply to anyone
practicing a medical profession in the UAE — it applies broadly to
medical and medicine-related professions as designated by ministerial
decision, not narrowly to doctors performing surgery alone. Determining
whether a specific incident actually constitutes a medical error under
the law is not left to the treating doctor or the patient to decide
informally — it goes through a structured review process rather than
being a purely civil "he said, she said" dispute from the outset.

The law also directly regulates specific high-sensitivity medical
scenarios rather than leaving them to general medical ethics guidance:
controls on performing surgical operations and gender-correction
procedures, an explicit prohibition on human cloning, rules on the
inappropriate installation of artificial organs, provisions covering
communicable disease cases, rules for performing resuscitation,
regulation of reproduction-related procedures, and the specific
circumstances in which an abortion is legally excluded from the general
abortion ban.

Because medical liability determinations under this law can feed into
both civil compensation claims and, in more serious cases, criminal
liability, how an incident gets classified early in this process has
consequences well beyond the specific patient dispute at hand.

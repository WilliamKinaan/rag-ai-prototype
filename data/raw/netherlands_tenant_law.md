# Dutch Residential Tenancy Law — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._

Dutch law gives most residential tenants strong protection against
termination (huurbescherming): a landlord generally cannot simply decide
to end a lease and ask the tenant to leave — ending a tenancy against the
tenant's wishes typically requires a specific legal ground and, in many
cases, going to court. This protection applies by default even to leases
that were originally agreed for a fixed term, unless a more limited
temporary-lease arrangement was validly used.

A notable feature of the Dutch system is the rent tribunal (huurcommissie),
a specialized body separate from the ordinary courts that tenants and
landlords can bring rent-level and service-charge disputes to. For much
of the regulated rental sector, the huurcommissie applies a points-based
assessment (puntensysteem) — based on factors like size, facilities, and
energy rating — to judge whether the asking rent is reasonable, and can
order a rent reduction if it isn't. Landlords also collect a security
deposit, generally expected to be returned promptly after move-out once
any damage has been assessed.

Because so much of this runs through the huurcommissie rather than a
judge, many rent-level disputes never need to reach a courtroom at all.

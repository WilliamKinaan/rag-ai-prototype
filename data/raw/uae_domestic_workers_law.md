# UAE Domestic Workers Law — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from Federal Decree-Law No. 9 of 2022 Concerning Domestic
Workers (in force 15 Dec 2022), MOHRE official materials._

Domestic workers — housemaids, drivers, nannies, and similar household
staff — are covered by a separate law from the general private-sector
Labour Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021), reflecting the
distinctive live-in, household-based nature of the work rather than
folding them into the ordinary employment framework. Federal Decree-Law
No. 9 of 2022 sets maximum working hours of 12 per day with at least 8
hours of rest, mandates at least one day off per week (with Friday
suggested as the preferred day), and entitles workers to 30 paid annual
leave days per year of service, cashable out if unused at the end of
employment.

Employers are required to provide suitable accommodation, food, and
medical care in addition to timely wages paid in full, and the law
explicitly bars employers from confiscating a domestic worker's personal
identification documents — a protection specifically targeting a
historically common source of abuse in this sector. The law sets a
minimum hiring age of 18, and separately prohibits discrimination on
grounds including race, gender, religion, and national origin, as well
as sexual harassment, forced labour, and any conduct that could amount
to human trafficking.

Disputes are handled through MOHRE in a broadly similar way to ordinary
private-sector labour complaints — a complaint is filed with the
ministry first, which can refer unresolved cases onward to the courts —
though the substantive rights and obligations involved differ from the
general Labour Law given domestic work's distinct nature.

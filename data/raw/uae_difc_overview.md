# DIFC (Dubai International Financial Centre) — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from Federal Decree-Law No. 35 of 2004 establishing the DIFC,
DIFC Employment Law No. 2 of 2019, and DIFC Courts official materials._

The DIFC is not just a business park with tax perks — it is a legally
autonomous financial free zone with its own common-law legal system,
built on English-law principles and operating in English, entirely
separate from the UAE's onshore civil-law courts and federal commercial
statutes. Companies registered in the DIFC are governed by DIFC company
law rather than the federal Commercial Companies Law, and disputes
involving DIFC-registered entities go to the DIFC Courts rather than the
Dubai onshore court system.

Employment within the DIFC follows the same separation: DIFC Employment
Law No. 2 of 2019 (as amended), not the federal Labour Law (Decree-Law
No. 33 of 2021), governs employment relationships for staff employed by
DIFC-registered entities, and the DIFC Courts have exclusive jurisdiction
over employment disputes arising there — MOHRE and the onshore Labour
Court have no role for an employment relationship genuinely inside the
DIFC. This is the same free-zone carve-out pattern that shows up
elsewhere in UAE law — data protection and banking regulation both
similarly exclude the DIFC from their federal frameworks.

The DIFC also runs its own Wills Service for non-Muslim residents (see
the companion doc on inheritance for expats), reflecting the same
underlying design: rather than a single legal system applying uniformly
across the UAE, several areas of law are genuinely different depending
on whether a person or company sits onshore or inside a common-law free
zone like the DIFC.

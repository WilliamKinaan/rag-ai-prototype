# Setting Up a Company in the UAE — Overview (Mainland)

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from Federal Decree-Law No. 32 of 2021 on Commercial Companies
(in force 2 Jan 2022) and the UAE Government's official platform
(u.ae)._

Onshore ("mainland") companies in the UAE are governed by Federal
Decree-Law No. 32 of 2021, which removed the long-standing requirement
that a UAE national hold at least 51% of the shares in most commercial
activities — founders can now generally own 100% of a mainland company
themselves. A defined list of "strategic impact" activities is excepted
from this: Cabinet Resolution No. 55 of 2021 names sectors such as
defence, banking, insurance, telecommunications, currency printing, and
hajj/umrah services, which still require Emirati participation or
special Cabinet approval regardless of the general reform.

Mainland licensing is handled by each emirate's own Department of
Economy (for example, Dubai's Department of Economy and Tourism, or
Abu Dhabi's ADDED) rather than a single federal registrar — the process
runs through trade-name reservation, initial approval, drafting the
memorandum of association, and finally license issuance once the
activity-specific conditions are met. A mainland license allows the
company to trade anywhere in the UAE and to bid on government
contracts, which free zone companies generally cannot do directly.

This mainland regime is distinct from the UAE's roughly 40-plus free
zones, each independently regulated by its own free zone authority
rather than the emirate's Department of Economy; free zone companies
have offered 100% foreign ownership since before the 2021 reform, but
historically could not trade directly onshore without a distributor —
a restriction newer "dual licensing" arrangements are starting to
relax.

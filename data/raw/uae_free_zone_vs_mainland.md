# UAE Free Zone vs Mainland Business Structuring — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from the UAE Government's official platform (u.ae) and Federal
Decree-Law No. 32 of 2021 on Commercial Companies._

A business setting up in the UAE generally chooses between a mainland
license, issued by the relevant emirate's Department of Economy, and a
free zone license, issued instead by that free zone's own independent
authority rather than the Department of Economy. Mainland companies can
trade anywhere in the UAE and bid on government contracts, and since
the 2021 reform can generally be 100% foreign-owned outside a short list
of strategic sectors. Free zone companies have offered 100% foreign
ownership since long before that reform, but historically could only
trade within their own zone or internationally — selling directly onto
the UAE mainland required going through a local distributor or setting
up a separate mainland branch.

Tax treatment also differs: mainland companies are subject to the
UAE's federal corporate tax once profits exceed the statutory
threshold, while free zone companies can continue to benefit from a 0%
rate on "qualifying income" provided they meet the free zone's specific
substance and activity conditions. Since reforms introduced in March
2025, "dual licensing" lets a free zone company also hold a mainland
license from the local Department of Economy, letting it trade directly
onshore while keeping its free zone status and benefits — narrowing a
gap that used to require a full separate mainland entity to bridge.

Two free zones go further still: the DIFC and ADGM are UAE financial
free zones that operate their own common-law legal systems and courts,
functioning largely independently of the onshore civil law courts used
elsewhere in the country.

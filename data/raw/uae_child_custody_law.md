# Child Custody and Guardianship in the UAE — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from the Personal Status Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 41 of
2024) as summarized by reputable UAE legal-industry sources._

UAE family law draws a distinction that's easy to conflate: custody
(hadana) is the day-to-day physical care and upbringing of a child,
while guardianship (wilaya) is the broader legal authority over the
child's affairs — decisions about education, medical treatment, travel
permission, and financial matters. These are not automatically held by
the same parent: in the traditional Sharia-based default, physical
custody is usually granted to the mother while legal guardianship stays
with the father, and the 2024 law specifically confirms educational
guardianship remains with the mother even under that split.

Recent reform moved this framework further than the older default: a
child is now given the ability to choose which parent they live with
once they reach 15 (rather than custody automatically transferring at a
fixed younger age as under older rules), custody itself can now extend
until the child turns 18, and religious-based restrictions on who may
hold custody have been removed. Throughout, the guiding legal standard
the courts apply is the "best interests of the child" rather than a
rigid formula that ignores the specifics of a given family.

Because custody and guardianship can sit with different parents
simultaneously, a parent who has physical custody does not automatically
have unilateral authority over decisions like enrolling the child in a
new school or approving international travel — those can still require
the guardian's involvement even though the child lives with the other
parent day to day.

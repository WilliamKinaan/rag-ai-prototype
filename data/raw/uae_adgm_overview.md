# ADGM (Abu Dhabi Global Market) — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from ADGM official legal-framework materials (adgm.com) and the
ADGM Rulebook._

ADGM is Abu Dhabi's equivalent to Dubai's DIFC — an international
financial centre with its own civil and commercial legal framework based
on English common law, established in 2015 to position Abu Dhabi as a
global business and finance hub. Three separate bodies split its
governance rather than one authority handling everything: the
Registration Authority (RA) handles business registration and licensing,
the Financial Services Regulatory Authority (FSRA) regulates financial
services activity against international standards, and the ADGM Courts
handle dispute resolution — a Court of First Instance and a Court of
Appeal, staffed with senior international judges.

Like the DIFC, ADGM operates independently of the UAE's onshore civil-law
courts and federal financial regulation (the Central Bank's governing
law explicitly excludes both financial free zones) — a business or
financial institution licensed and regulated within ADGM answers to the
FSRA and ADGM Courts, not to the UAE Central Bank or the onshore Abu
Dhabi Judicial Department for matters arising within the zone. The ADGM
Courts have also pushed further into a fully digital court process than
most comparable systems, aiming for an end-to-end digital judicial
experience rather than a traditional paper-based one.

Because Abu Dhabi and Dubai each run their own separate common-law
financial free zone (ADGM and the DIFC respectively) alongside the
onshore federal and emirate-level systems, "which UAE law applies" can
genuinely have three different answers — onshore, DIFC, or ADGM —
depending on where an entity is actually registered, not just which
emirate it happens to be located in.

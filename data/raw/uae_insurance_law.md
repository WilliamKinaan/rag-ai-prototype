# UAE Insurance Regulation — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Originally enacted as Federal Decree-Law No. 48 of 2023 Regulating
Insurance Activities; now consolidated into Federal Decree-Law No. 6 of
2025 Regarding the Central Bank, Regulation of Financial Institutions
and Activities, and Insurance Business — see the companion doc on UAE
banking regulation for the same law's banking side. Sourced from
the Central Bank of the UAE (CBUAE) Rulebook and reputable legal-industry
summaries._

Insurance regulation in the UAE moved from a standalone regulator to the
Central Bank: the old UAE Insurance Authority was merged into the CBUAE,
and the 2023 reform (now folded into the broader 2025 consolidated law)
brought licensing, solvency, and conduct oversight for insurers and
insurance brokers all under the same regulator that already supervises
banks — a structural change from the earlier arrangement where insurance
had its own separate authority.

Insurance brokers specifically saw tighter licensing conditions under
this framework, with the CBUAE directly responsible for licensing every
broker operating in the UAE rather than that role being split across
multiple bodies. Enforcement powers were also strengthened relative to
the prior regime: violations can lead to suspension or outright
revocation of a license, in addition to fines and other administrative
penalties — giving the regulator more than just a fine as its response
to serious non-compliance.

As with UAE banking regulation generally, this framework does not extend
into the DIFC or ADGM financial free zones, which license and supervise
insurers operating within those zones under their own separate
regulators rather than the CBUAE.

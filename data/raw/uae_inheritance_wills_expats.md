# Inheritance and Wills for Non-Muslim Expats in the UAE — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from Federal Personal Status Law No. 41 of 2022 and DIFC Courts'
official materials on the DIFC Wills Service._

Absent a valid, registered will, a deceased person's UAE assets are
generally distributed under Sharia-based inheritance rules set out in
Federal Personal Status Law No. 41 of 2022 — a default that can produce
very different outcomes from the inheritance rules of an expat's home
country. The same federal law, however, allows a non-Muslim foreign
national to instead elect for their home country's inheritance law to
govern their will, provided the will is properly drafted and registered
rather than left informal or unregistered.

In practice, the most established route for doing this is the DIFC
Wills Service, a joint initiative of the Dubai Government and the DIFC
Courts (established under Resolution No. 4 of 2014, with its authority
reaffirmed by Dubai Law No. 15 of 2017). It lets non-Muslim residents
aged 21 or over register a common-law will covering UAE property,
business shares, financial accounts, or the guardianship of minor
children, all under common-law principles rather than the default
Sharia-based framework. Several narrower will types exist alongside the
full will — for example, a guardianship-only will or a property-only
will.

Once the DIFC Courts issue a probate order on such a will, it is
enforceable across the whole UAE without needing a separate onshore
court proceeding. Abu Dhabi's Judicial Department separately runs its
own comparable non-Muslim wills registry for that emirate.

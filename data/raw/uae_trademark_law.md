# UAE Trademark Law — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from Federal Decree-Law No. 36 of 2021 on Trademarks (in force
1 Mar 2022) and its executive regulation (Cabinet Resolution No. 57 of
2022), Ministry of Economy & Tourism official materials._

Federal Decree-Law No. 36 of 2021 modernized UAE trademark protection,
expanding what can actually be registered as a trademark beyond
traditional word and logo marks to include "non-traditional" signs such
as 3D shapes, sounds, holograms, and even smells — a broader scope than
many trademark regimes still limited to visual marks. It also introduced
multi-class filing, letting an applicant cover several categories of
goods or services in a single application rather than filing separately
for each class, and clarified the opposition procedure for challenging
a pending registration.

Trademarks are registered and administered centrally by the Ministry of
Economy, which maintains a public Trademarks Register recording the
mark, owner details, and the goods or services it covers. Enforcement
spans civil remedies (injunctions and damages against infringers),
criminal penalties for deliberate counterfeiting, and border-control
measures allowing UAE Customs to seize suspected counterfeit goods
before they even enter general circulation — a combination aimed
squarely at the UAE's role as a regional trade and re-export hub.

Registration is territorial to the UAE itself; a trademark registered
elsewhere (including in a UAE free zone's own separate business
registry) does not automatically extend protection to the rest of the
UAE without a corresponding Ministry of Economy registration.

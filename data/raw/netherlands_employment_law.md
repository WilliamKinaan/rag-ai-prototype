# Dutch Employment Law — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._

Dutch employment contracts are generally either permanent (vast contract)
or temporary (tijdelijk contract), and it's common for a relationship to
start with one or more temporary contracts before converting to a
permanent one. Contracts usually include a short trial period (proeftijd)
at the very start, during which either the employer or the employee can
end the relationship immediately, without notice and without needing
anyone else's approval.

Outside that initial trial period, an employer generally cannot simply
dismiss an employee on their own decision. Depending on the reason,
the employer must first obtain permission — either from the government
employment agency (UWV), typically for redundancy or long-term illness,
or from the sub-district court (kantonrechter), typically for
performance or personal-conduct reasons — before the dismissal can take
effect. A dismissal can also happen by mutual agreement, recorded in a
settlement agreement (vaststellingsovereenkomst), which skips the
approval step entirely. Employees dismissed after a sufficiently long
contract are often entitled to a statutory transition payment
(transitievergoeding).

Many sectors are additionally covered by collective labour agreements
(cao's), which can set their own rules on pay scales, notice periods, and
other conditions on top of the general statutory framework.

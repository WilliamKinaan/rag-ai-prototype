# UAE Bounced Cheque Law — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from Federal Decree-Law No. 14 of 2020, amending the Commercial
Transactions Law (Federal Law No. 18 of 1993), in force 2 Jan 2022, as
summarized by reputable UAE legal-industry sources._

Until this reform, a bounced cheque in the UAE was historically treated
as a criminal matter, and cheque-related arrests were a well-known risk
for residents and businesses alike. Federal Decree-Law No. 14 of 2020
decriminalized the ordinary case: if a cheque bounces simply because of
insufficient funds, an incorrect signature made without malicious
intent, or a closed account, the drawer no longer faces criminal
liability for that alone — a significant shift from the pre-2022
position, where the same facts could lead to prosecution.

Criminal exposure has not disappeared entirely, though: cheques issued
with fraudulent intent, cheques that are forged or falsified, and
cheques drawn on accounts that never existed can still trigger criminal
charges, so decriminalization applies to the ordinary insufficient-funds
scenario rather than to cheque misuse generally. On the civil side, the
reform strengthened the cheque holder's position by treating a bounced
cheque as an "executive instrument" — meaning the holder can go straight
to enforcement (attaching the drawer's assets or bank accounts through
an execution judge) without first having to win a separate lawsuit to
prove the debt.

This combination — lighter criminal exposure but faster civil
enforcement — is a distinctive feature of the UAE's post-2022 cheque
regime compared to jurisdictions that either criminalize bounced
cheques broadly or treat them as ordinary unsecured debt.

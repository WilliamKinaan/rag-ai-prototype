# Setting Up a Company in France — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._

Two of the most common company types for founders in France are the SARL
(société à responsabilité limitée), a private limited company whose
ownership is divided into shares called parts sociales, and the SAS
(société par actions simplifiée), which offers founders much more
flexibility to design their own governance rules in the company's
articles of association (statuts) rather than following a more fixed
statutory template. There is generally no legally mandated minimum share
capital for either structure — founders can set it based on their needs.

To formally exist, a new company must be registered in the Trade and
Companies Register (RCS — Registre du Commerce et des Sociétés), a filing
handled through the commercial court registry (greffe du tribunal de
commerce) or an equivalent online formalities service. For a standard
incorporation, the articles of association are typically signed as a
private document between the founders (acte sous seing privé) — going
before a notary is usually only necessary in specific situations, such as
when real estate is being contributed to the company's capital.

Once registered, the company receives a unique identification number and
can begin operating, opening bank accounts, and entering into contracts
in its own name.

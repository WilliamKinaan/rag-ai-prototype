# UAE Emiratisation Quotas (Nafis) — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from UAE Cabinet decisions on private-sector Emiratisation and
the UAE Government's official platform (u.ae), implemented through the
Nafis program._

Private-sector companies above a set size threshold — establishments
with 50 or more employees — are required to increase the share of
Emirati nationals in skilled roles by a fixed percentage each year, with
a cumulative target building toward a larger overall increase by a set
deadline, rather than a one-time hiring requirement. This is a headcount
obligation tied to skilled-role classifications specifically, not a
blanket quota across every job type in the company.

Non-compliant establishments face an escalating monthly financial
contribution for every Emirati position they fall short of the target
by, with the per-position amount increasing each year rather than
staying fixed — a structure designed to make delay progressively more
costly rather than a one-off penalty a company might simply budget for
once. Compliance is monitored continuously, not just at an annual
audit: a federal digital platform tracks workforce composition,
Nafis registration, and related signals (including Wage Protection
System data) for every covered employer in real time.

The Nafis program itself is the supporting side of this policy rather
than the enforcement side — it provides incentives, training, and
competitiveness support to help Emirati jobseekers meet the skill
requirements employers are being pushed to hire for, so the quota
obligation and the Nafis support programs are designed to work together
rather than as two unrelated initiatives.

# Off-Plan Property Registration in Dubai (Oqood) — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from Dubai Law No. 13 of 2008 (Off-Plan Registration Law) and
Executive Council Resolution No. 6 of 2010, Dubai Land Department (DLD)
official materials. Complements [[uae_real_estate_escrow_law]], which
covers how buyer payments themselves are protected during construction._

Buying a property that doesn't exist yet creates an obvious problem —
what actually proves a buyer's claim to a specific unit before there's a
building to hold title to? Dubai's answer is Oqood ("contracts"), a
DLD-run interim property register that records the sale agreement
between developer and buyer for units still under construction, distinct
from the final title deed issued only once the project completes and
units are handed over.

Registration isn't optional or just good practice: under Law No. 13 of
2008, developers must register a project with the DLD before they can
even market or sell units in it, and once a buyer signs a sale and
purchase agreement, that specific contract must itself be registered
through the Oqood system within 90 days. A registered Oqood certificate
serves as official government-recognized proof of the buyer's interim
ownership interest, and — importantly — it prevents the same unit from
being sold to a second buyer, since the register itself blocks a
duplicate registration once one buyer already holds it.

This interim-register mechanism is separate from, but works alongside,
the escrow-account protection for the buyer's actual payments (Dubai Law
No. 8 of 2007): Oqood protects the buyer's legal claim to the specific
unit, while the escrow account protects the money paid toward it — a
buyer's interests during an off-plan purchase depend on both mechanisms
functioning correctly, not just one or the other.

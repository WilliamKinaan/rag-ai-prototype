# French Residential Tenancy Law — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._

Residential leases in France (bail) commonly come in two flavors:
unfurnished (bail vide), which typically runs for a longer standard term
and renews unless properly terminated, and furnished (bail meublé), which
runs shorter and offers the landlord and tenant somewhat more flexibility.
Landlords generally collect a security deposit (dépôt de garantie) at the
start of the lease, capped relative to the rent, which must be returned
within a set period after the tenant moves out, minus any documented
deductions for damage or unpaid charges.

A distinctive tenant protection is the winter eviction moratorium (trêve
hivernale): even where a landlord has obtained a court order to evict a
tenant, physical eviction is generally suspended during the winter
months, regardless of how the underlying dispute is proceeding. Landlords
also face restrictions on ending a lease early or refusing renewal
outside of specific legal grounds, and must generally give advance
written notice.

Disputes between landlords and tenants — over deposits, repairs, or
termination — are primarily resolved through the ordinary civil courts
(tribunal judiciaire), though an optional conciliation commission can be
used first to try to resolve smaller disputes without going to court.

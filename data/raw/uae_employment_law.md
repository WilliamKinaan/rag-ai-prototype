# UAE Private-Sector Employment Law — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021 on the Regulation of
Labour Relations (in force 2 Feb 2022) and its executive regulations, as
published by the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation (MOHRE)
and uaelegislation.gov.ae._

Private-sector employment in the UAE is governed federally by Decree-Law
No. 33 of 2021, which abolished the older open-ended ("unlimited")
contract entirely — every contract must now be a fixed-term agreement,
renewable, alongside newer full-time, part-time, temporary, and flexible
work models. A probation period of up to six months is permitted, during
which either side can end the relationship with shorter notice than
applies afterward. Outside probation, ending a contract requires the
statutory notice period (30 to 90 days, as set in the contract) — the
employer does not need prior court or ministry approval to dismiss, but
a dismissal found to be "arbitrary" (without a legitimate work-related
reason) exposes the employer to compensation.

Employees who believe they were dismissed unfairly, or have any labour
dispute over wages, gratuity, or contract terms, must first file a
complaint with MOHRE, which attempts conciliation; if that fails, MOHRE
refers the case directly to the specialized Labour Court for a ruling,
rather than the employee suing on their own initiative. Most employees
are also entitled to an end-of-service gratuity on completion of at
least one year of service, calculated as a set number of days' basic
salary per year worked, tiered by length of service.

Free zones with their own legal systems — notably the DIFC and ADGM —
apply their own separate employment regulations instead of Decree-Law
No. 33 for staff employed within those zones, so the applicable regime
depends on where the employing entity is licensed, not just on being
"in the UAE."

# UAE Traffic Law — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from the UAE's federal traffic law (as amended, in force since
1 July 2017) as summarized on the UAE Government's official platform
(u.ae) and Roads and Transport Authority (RTA) materials._

Traffic law in the UAE is unified at the federal level and applies
across all seven emirates, though day-to-day enforcement is carried out
by each emirate's own traffic police (in Dubai, in coordination with the
RTA). Violations combine two separate consequences: a cash fine, and
"black points" recorded against the driver's licence itself rather than
against the vehicle — a distinction that matters because black points
follow the individual even if they change cars, and clear automatically
after a year of violation-free driving.

Accumulating 24 black points within a single year triggers an automatic
licence suspension nationwide, not just a local penalty — for drivers
21 or older, a first suspension runs 3 months, though it can sometimes
be shortened by paying a fee and completing a one-day rehabilitation
course. Penalties scale sharply with severity: using a phone while
driving draws a modest fine and points, while reckless driving or
extreme speeding can draw thousands of dirhams in fines, over 20 black
points in one violation, and vehicle impoundment for weeks at a time.

Because fines and points attach automatically at the moment of
detection (including via speed cameras), disputing a traffic fine
generally means filing a formal objection with the relevant traffic
department rather than raising it as a defence later in an unrelated
court case.

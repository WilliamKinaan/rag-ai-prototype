# French Employment Law — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._

French employment contracts are typically either open-ended (CDI —
contrat à durée indéterminée) or fixed-term (CDD — contrat à durée
déterminée), with the CDI treated as the default and CDD reserved for
specific, temporary situations. Most contracts include an initial
probation period (période d'essai) during which either party can end the
relationship more easily, though even then some notice is generally
expected.

Outside the probation period, an employer does not need prior approval
from a court or authority to dismiss an employee — the employer can end
the contract, provided a valid ground (motif) is given and proper
procedure is followed, and the dismissal takes effect. If the employee
believes the dismissal was unfair or improperly handled, they can
challenge it afterward before the labor court (conseil de prud'hommes),
which will rule on the dispute and can award compensation if the
dismissal is found unjustified.

Many employees are also covered by sector-wide collective bargaining
agreements (conventions collectives), which can set additional rules on
notice periods, severance, and working conditions beyond the general
labor code. Employers of a certain size must also consult employee
representative bodies before certain collective decisions.

# UAE Cybercrime Law — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from Federal Decree-Law No. 34 of 2021 on Combatting Rumours
and Cybercrimes (in force 2 Jan 2022), as summarized by official and
reputable legal-industry sources._

Federal Decree-Law No. 34 of 2021 is a standalone law dealing
specifically with online and IT-related offences, separate from the
general Penal Code (Federal Decree-Law No. 31 of 2021). It covers
hacking and unauthorized access to government or private systems,
impersonation of websites, social media accounts, or emails, electronic
fraud, and the spread of rumours or false information that could harm
public order, national security, or public health — a notably broad
category compared to narrower "computer misuse" statutes elsewhere.

Penalties scale with the harm targeted: offences against government
systems or critical infrastructure attract the heaviest sentences, with
some categories carrying at least 7 years' imprisonment and fines
between AED 250,000 and AED 1,500,000, well above what equivalent
private-sector hacking offences typically draw. The law also covers
narrower everyday issues such as unauthorized recording or publication
of another person's photos or private conversations, and online content
considered defamatory or an invasion of privacy — areas that in many
other jurisdictions would be handled as civil claims rather than
criminal cybercrime charges.

Enforcement sits with the police and Public Prosecution rather than a
separate cyber-regulator, though the Telecommunications and Digital
Government Regulatory Authority (TDRA) also plays a role in the
broader digital-safety framework this law sits within.

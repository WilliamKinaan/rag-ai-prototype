# UAE Climate Change Law — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from Federal Decree-Law No. 11 of 2024 on the Reduction of
Climate Change Effects (in force 30 May 2025), as summarized by
reputable ESG and legal-industry sources._

Federal Decree-Law No. 11 of 2024 made the UAE the first country in the
MENA region to legislate corporate climate accountability directly,
rather than addressing emissions only through voluntary commitments or
sector-specific rules. It applies to public and private entities whose
operations release greenhouse gases into the atmosphere — including,
notably, entities operating in free zones, which many other UAE
regulatory frameworks (banking, data protection) explicitly carve out.

Non-compliance is backed by real financial exposure rather than a
symbolic penalty: fines range from AED 50,000 up to AED 2 million per
violation, and a repeated violation within a two-year window doubles the
fine rather than resetting to the same base amount — a structure meant
to punish a pattern of non-compliance more than an isolated lapse. For
more severe or continued breaches, the law also allows administrative
measures beyond fines: restrictions on business operations, suspension
of licenses, and mandatory corrective environmental measures.

The law's stated goals go beyond pure enforcement — managing emissions,
strengthening ecosystem resilience, and encouraging climate-related
innovation and research are named objectives alongside the compliance
regime, positioning it as part of a broader sustainability push rather
than a narrowly punitive emissions law.

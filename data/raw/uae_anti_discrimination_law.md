# UAE Anti-Discrimination and Hate Speech Law — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Current law is Federal Decree-Law No. 34 of 2023, which abrogated the
earlier Federal Decree-Law No. 2 of 2015 (as previously amended by
Federal Decree-Law No. 11 of 2019) — sourced from reputable UAE
legal-industry summaries of both the original and current framework._

The UAE's anti-discrimination framework criminalizes acts that
discriminate against, or stoke hatred toward, individuals or groups on
grounds including religion, caste, creed, doctrine, race, colour, or
ethnic origin — and it extends specifically to expression, not just
physical conduct: publicly insulting a religion, or expressing content
online, in print, or through broadcast media that incites religious or
ethnic hatred, falls within scope alongside more conventional
discrimination.

Penalties under the original 2015 framework were substantial by
comparison to many general speech or discrimination laws elsewhere —
imprisonment terms spanning a wide range up to fifteen years, and fines
reaching into the millions of dirhams for the most serious offenses —
reflecting how seriously the UAE treats threats to intercommunal
tolerance as a matter of public order, not merely a civil dispute
between the parties involved.

The framework has been updated more than once since 2015 (most recently
replaced outright by Federal Decree-Law No. 34 of 2023), which is a
useful reminder for this whole area of UAE law generally: statutes
addressing online expression and discrimination have moved faster than
many other legal domains, so confirming which version of a law is
currently in force matters more here than in more slowly-evolving areas
like property or contract law.

# Property Mortgages in Dubai — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from Dubai Law No. 14 of 2008 Concerning Mortgages, Dubai Land
Department (DLD) official materials._

A property mortgage in Dubai isn't valid merely because the borrower and
lender signed a private agreement — Law No. 14 of 2008 makes registration
with the Dubai Land Department a condition of validity itself, and any
side agreement purporting to waive that requirement is void, not just
unenforceable in a dispute. Once registered, the mortgage attaches to
the property's title as a formal encumbrance, which is what actually
stops the owner from selling or transferring the property without the
lender's consent while the loan remains outstanding — it's the
registration, not the private loan contract, that has this effect
against third parties.

The mortgage application itself must capture specific details on the
standard DLD form — the property, its value, the value of the debt
being secured, the mortgage term, and the identifying and residence
details of both mortgagor and mortgagee — and gets recorded against
either the standard Real Estate Register or the Interim Real Estate
Register, depending on whether the property already has a full title
deed or is still an off-plan interest (see the companion doc on Oqood
interim registration for that distinction).

Where more than one mortgage is registered against the same property,
priority between them is determined simply by the order of registration
— the serial number assigned by the DLD — rather than by the date the
underlying loan agreements were signed, which matters in a default
scenario when multiple lenders are competing over the same collateral.

# UAE Wage Protection System (WPS) — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from MOHRE regulations on the Wage Protection System and the
UAE Government's official platform (u.ae)._

The Wage Protection System is an electronic salary-transfer requirement,
not a voluntary payroll convenience: MOHRE-registered private-sector
employers must pay wages through WPS or another ministry-approved system,
routed via banks and financial institutions licensed by the Central
Bank, rather than in cash or by informal transfer. Every transfer is
recorded and checked automatically against each employee's registered
contract terms — violations are flagged by the system itself, with no
employee complaint needed to trigger enforcement, unlike most labour
disputes which start with a complaint.

An establishment is treated as compliant only if it transfers a high
percentage of total wages due by the monthly due date; falling short
exposes the employer to escalating penalties, which can include fines,
restrictions on issuing new work permits, and other administrative
measures against the establishment rather than only against the
individual case at hand. The due date itself is fixed relative to the
prior month's wages, so a delay is measured automatically rather than
left to interpretation.

WPS runs alongside the general Labour Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of
2021) rather than replacing it — it is specifically the enforcement
mechanism for the underlying legal obligation to pay wages on time, giving
MOHRE a real-time compliance signal instead of relying solely on
after-the-fact employee complaints.

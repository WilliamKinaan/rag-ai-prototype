# Foreign Real Estate Ownership in Dubai — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from Dubai Law No. 7 of 2006 on Real Estate Registration and
Regulation No. 3 of 2006 on designated areas, as summarized by Dubai
Land Department (DLD) official materials._

Dubai Law No. 7 of 2006 is what allows foreign nationals — not just UAE
or GCC nationals — to own real estate at all in Dubai, but only within
specific "designated areas" named by the Ruler of Dubai under
Regulation No. 3 of 2006, rather than anywhere in the emirate. Within a
designated area, a foreign buyer can hold full freehold ownership, or
alternatively a usufruct or long lease of up to 99 years; outside those
areas, ownership generally stays reserved for UAE/GCC nationals, and a
foreign buyer's options are limited to a long leasehold at most.

The list of designated areas has been expanded repeatedly since 2006 and
now covers 60-plus communities — including well-known districts like
Palm Jumeirah, Downtown Dubai, Dubai Marina, and Business Bay — but is
not exhaustive of every popular-sounding neighborhood, and new
communities are added as new master-developments launch. Because of
this, confirming that a specific building or plot actually falls inside
a currently designated area (via the Dubai Land Department) before
committing to a purchase is a standard, necessary step rather than an
optional precaution.

Ownership itself is only finalized once title is registered with the
Dubai Land Department; an unregistered sale and purchase agreement is
not sufficient by itself to establish legal ownership against third
parties.

# UAE Court System and Civil Litigation — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from reputable UAE legal-industry summaries of the onshore
court structure and the UAE Government's official platform (u.ae)._

Onshore UAE litigation runs through three tiers: the Court of First
Instance, the Court of Appeal, and the Court of Cassation (called the
Federal Supreme Court for federal-level matters). The Court of First
Instance hears the case on the merits and is itself split by claim
value — smaller claims (commonly cited around AED 10 million or below)
go to a single judge in a "Minor" circuit, while larger claims go to a
three-judge panel in a "Major" circuit, meaning the same court can
operate quite differently depending on how much money is at stake.

The Court of Appeal can review both the facts and the law from a First
Instance judgment and may uphold, amend, or completely overturn the
decision — a broader scope of review than many appellate courts
elsewhere, which often restrict themselves to legal error only. The
Court of Cassation, by contrast, is generally limited to reviewing
questions of law rather than re-examining the facts, and as a general
rule only hears cases where the amount in dispute exceeds AED 500,000
(unless the case raises a non-monetary issue), which filters out most
smaller commercial disputes from reaching this final tier.

Each emirate largely runs its own local court system for onshore
matters (Dubai Courts, Abu Dhabi Judicial Department, and so on) under
this same general three-tier structure, alongside the separate common-law
court systems of the DIFC and ADGM financial free zones, which operate
independently of these onshore civil-law courts entirely.

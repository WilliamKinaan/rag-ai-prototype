# Dubai Real Estate Escrow (Off-Plan Buyer Protection) — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from Dubai Law No. 8 of 2007 Concerning Escrow Accounts for
Real Estate Development, overseen by the Real Estate Regulatory Agency
(RERA), a branch of the Dubai Land Department._

Buying an off-plan (under-construction) property in Dubai involves a
buyer-protection mechanism most resale or ready-property purchases
don't need: Dubai Law No. 8 of 2007 requires developers to deposit every
payment collected from off-plan buyers into a dedicated escrow account
for that specific project, opened only after RERA approval, rather than
letting the developer use buyer payments freely across multiple
projects or for unrelated expenses. The account is managed by a
"Trustee" — typically a major bank licensed by the UAE Central Bank —
not by the developer itself.

Funds in the escrow account are legally ring-fenced: creditors of the
developer cannot attach or seize the payments held there, and money is
released to the developer only in stages, tied to independently
certified construction milestones being reached, rather than as a lump
sum on signing. Even after the project reaches completion, the trustee
must hold back 5% of the account's total value, releasing that final
retained amount to the developer only one year after units are formally
registered in the buyers' names — a further cushion covering
post-handover defects or disputes.

Because the escrow requirement is project-specific, a buyer's practical
safeguard is confirming — via RERA/DLD — that a given project's escrow
account is properly registered and active, rather than assuming every
Dubai developer follows the rules by default.

# UAE Arbitration Law — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from Federal Law No. 6 of 2018 on Arbitration, as summarized by
reputable UAE legal-industry sources._

Before 2018, arbitration in onshore UAE was governed only by a short
chapter within the general Civil Procedure Code (Federal Law No. 11 of
1992), which many practitioners considered thin and outdated relative to
international norms. Federal Law No. 6 of 2018 replaced that chapter
with a full standalone arbitration statute modeled closely on the
UNCITRAL Model Law, bringing onshore UAE arbitration procedure much
closer to what is used in established international arbitration seats.

The law recognizes core modern-arbitration principles such as
"severability" — meaning an arbitration clause within a contract can
remain valid and enforceable even if the rest of the contract is later
found invalid or is disputed — and generally limits the onshore courts'
role to a supervisory one (for example, appointing arbitrators when
parties can't agree, or enforcing/setting aside awards) rather than
letting a court re-decide the substance of an arbitrated dispute. The
Dubai International Arbitration Centre (DIAC) is the leading institution
administering arbitrations under this law, particularly for
construction, real estate, and general commercial contracts.

This onshore federal regime sits alongside — but separately from — the
independent arbitration frameworks of the DIFC and ADGM free zones,
which have their own arbitration laws and their own affiliated
institutions (such as the DIFC-LCIA), so which regime applies depends on
where the arbitration is seated, not simply on the dispute being "in the
UAE."

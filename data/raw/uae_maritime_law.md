# UAE Maritime Law — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from Federal Decree-Law No. 43 of 2023 Concerning the Maritime
Law (in force 29 Mar 2024, replacing Federal Law No. 26 of 1981), as
summarized by reputable maritime and legal-industry sources._

The 2023 Maritime Law is a full replacement of the UAE's maritime code,
not an amendment layered on the 1981 original — a genuinely large gap
given how much shipping practice and international maritime norms
changed over more than four decades. It applies broadly to marine
structures, ships, and other maritime means engaged in transportation
and navigation, covering registration, ship mortgages, collision and
salvage rules, and limitation of liability, among other areas a general
commercial or civil law wouldn't reach.

Several changes specifically modernize ship-related transactions: vessel
registration can now happen digitally rather than only through paper
filings, and — notably — vessels that are still under construction, or
that are only under charter rather than owned outright, can now be
registered, which the old law did not clearly permit. On the dispute
side, the time limits (time bars) for bringing collision and towage
claims were shortened and clarified, and the law now accepts security
from a P&I Club (a shipowner's mutual insurance association) as
sufficient to lift a vessel arrest, rather than requiring a bank
guarantee or cash deposit in every case.

Because ship arrest and security rules directly affect how quickly a
vessel can resume operating after a dispute arises, these procedural
changes matter as much in practice as the substantive registration and
liability provisions, even though they attract less attention than the
headline "new maritime code" framing.

# UAE Consumer Protection Law — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from Federal Law No. 15 of 2020 on Consumer Protection (as
amended by Federal Decree-Law No. 5 of 2023), Ministry of Economy &
Tourism official materials._

Federal Law No. 15 of 2020 sets out consumer rights around the quality,
safety, and advertised pricing of goods and services, and applies
broadly — including to free zones and to e-commerce transactions where
the supplier is registered in the UAE, which is wider reach than many
comparable consumer laws that carve out free zones or online-only
sellers. It establishes a Higher Committee for Consumer Protection and
imposes obligations directly on suppliers, advertisers, and trade
agents, not just on the final retailer a consumer deals with.

Core protections include the right to accurate information about a
product or service before purchase, the right to a fair price matching
what was advertised, safety standards for goods sold, and rules around
misleading advertising. The law also gives the authorities emergency
powers to intervene directly in the market — for example capping prices
or restricting the sale of certain goods — during situations like public
health emergencies or supply shortages, a tool most Western consumer
protection regimes handle through separate emergency legislation rather
than folding into the ordinary consumer law itself.

Complaints are generally handled first through the Ministry of Economy
& Tourism (or the relevant emirate's economic department) rather than
starting directly in court, giving regulators a chance to mediate or
penalize non-compliant traders before a consumer needs to litigate.

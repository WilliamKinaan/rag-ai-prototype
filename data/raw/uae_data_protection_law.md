# UAE Personal Data Protection Law — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 on the Protection of
Personal Data (PDPL), in force 2 Jan 2022, as summarized on the UAE
Government's official platform (u.ae)._

The PDPL is the UAE's first cross-sector federal data protection law,
giving individuals rights over their personal data — including access,
correction, erasure, and objecting to processing or direct marketing —
and imposing obligations on organizations that control or process that
data: a lawful basis for processing, breach notification duties, a
Data Protection Officer in certain higher-risk cases, and restrictions
on transferring personal data outside the UAE unless the destination
offers an adequate level of protection or another approved safeguard is
in place. A federal UAE Data Office oversees enforcement.

The PDPL explicitly does not cover everything: it excludes government
data, security and judicial-authority data, personal health data, and
personal financial/credit data, each of which is instead addressed by
its own separate sector-specific legislation. It also does not apply
inside financial free zones — the DIFC and ADGM instead operate their
own independent data protection regimes (the DIFC Data Protection Law
and the ADGM Data Protection Regulations), which are modeled more
closely on the EU's GDPR and are enforced by each free zone's own
regulator rather than the federal UAE Data Office.

Because of this split, which data protection law applies to a given
company in the UAE depends on where that company is registered — onshore
mainland, DIFC, or ADGM — not simply on being physically located in the
country.

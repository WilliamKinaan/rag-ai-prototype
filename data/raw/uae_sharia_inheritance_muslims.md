# Sharia Inheritance (Fara'id) for Muslims in the UAE — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from the Personal Status Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 41 of 2024,
replacing Federal Law No. 28 of 2005) as summarized by reputable UAE
legal-industry sources. Contrast with [[uae_inheritance_wills_expats]] —
that doc covers the separate non-Muslim opt-out route via DIFC wills._

Unlike the civil, will-driven succession route available to non-Muslims
(see the companion doc on DIFC wills), a Muslim's estate in the UAE is
distributed according to Fara'id — a system of fixed inheritance shares
set out in the Personal Status Law — and this applies automatically to
all Muslim estates with UAE-based assets, regardless of the deceased's
nationality. The shares are not something the deceased can freely
redesign in a will; they follow predetermined formulas (fard) based on
each surviving relative's relationship to the deceased.

A frequently misunderstood feature is the "2:1 rule": in many
relationship categories, a male heir's fixed share is set at twice that
of a female heir in an equivalent position — a rule Fara'id treats as
fixed rather than adjustable case by case. A Muslim's freedom to
override this through a will is deliberately limited: at most one-third
of the estate can be freely bequeathed outside the fixed heirs, and a
bequest to someone who is already a fixed legal heir (a son or a wife,
for example) generally requires the consent of all the other legal
heirs after death to take effect — it isn't valid by the will alone.

Because this framework applies by default and isn't opt-out the way the
non-Muslim civil track is, Muslim residents who want a different
outcome for the freely-disposable third of their estate need a
properly drafted will addressing that portion specifically, rather than
assuming a will alone can redirect the fixed shares.

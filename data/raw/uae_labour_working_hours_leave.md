# UAE Working Hours, Overtime, and Leave — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021 and its executive
regulations, MOHRE official materials. Complements the companion doc on
UAE employment law, which covers contract types, probation, and
termination._

Standard working hours under the federal Labour Law are capped at 8
hours a day or 48 hours a week, with a specific, legally mandated
reduction to 6 hours a day during Ramadan — not a custom many employers
independently choose, but a statutory requirement tied to the calendar
rather than a fixed working-hours rule year-round. Overtime beyond the
standard hours is capped at 2 hours a day and must be paid at a premium
rather than straight time: 125% of the normal hourly rate for daytime
overtime, rising to 150% for overtime worked at night (between 10 PM and
4 AM), reflecting a higher premium for night work specifically.

Friday remains the default weekly rest day, though an employer can shift
an employee to a different rest day with MOHRE approval rather than
unilaterally; an employee required to work on their rest day is entitled
to either a substitute day off or 150% overtime pay for that day, not
simply their normal rate. Annual leave accrues to 30 calendar days per
year once an employee passes one year of service, with a prorated 2
days per month for those between 6 and 12 months in — so leave
entitlement genuinely differs by tenure rather than being a flat number
from day one.

Sick leave and maternity leave follow their own tiered structures rather
than a single flat entitlement: sick leave totals 90 days a year, split
into 15 days at full pay, the next 30 at half pay, and the remaining 45
unpaid; maternity leave is 60 days total, 45 at full pay and 15 at half
pay — both designed so the paid proportion tapers rather than cutting
off abruptly partway through.

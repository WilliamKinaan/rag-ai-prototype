# UAE Electronic Transactions and Trust Services Law — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from Federal Decree-Law No. 46 of 2021 on Electronic
Transactions and Trust Services (replacing Federal Law No. 1 of 2006 on
E-Commerce) and its executive regulation (Cabinet Resolution No. 28 of
2023), as summarized on the UAE Government's official platform (u.ae)._

The 2021 law gives electronic documents and electronic signatures the
same legal standing as their paper equivalents — a signature made
electronically under this framework is as binding as one made by hand,
which matters for any contract, application, or filing a business or
individual wants to complete without physical paperwork. This replaced
an older 2006 e-commerce law that predated most of the digital-signature
and trust-service infrastructure the current law now regulates in
detail.

"Trust services" is the newer, more specific concept this law
introduces: licensed providers can issue things like digital
certificates and time-stamping services that carry legal weight,
creating a regulated market for the infrastructure that makes electronic
signatures verifiable and trustworthy — not just permitting electronic
signatures in principle, but building out who is allowed to certify
them. The 2023 executive regulation extended this further by working
toward trust services that carry the same legal status across the whole
UAE, rather than needing separate recognition emirate by emirate.

Because this is a federal law aimed at digital-economy infrastructure
generally, it sits alongside — rather than inside — sector-specific
digital rules like the Cybercrime Law or the PDPL: electronic signature
validity, cybercrime, and data protection are each governed by their own
separate UAE statute rather than one combined "digital law."

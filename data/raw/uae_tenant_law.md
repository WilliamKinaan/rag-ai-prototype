# UAE Residential Tenancy Law — Overview (Dubai)

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from Dubai Law No. 26 of 2007 (as amended by Law No. 33 of
2008) and related Dubai Land Department / RERA materials. Tenancy law
in the UAE is regulated at the emirate level, not federally — Abu Dhabi,
Sharjah, and other emirates each have their own separate rules; this
summary covers Dubai only._

Unlike the federal laws that govern employment or company formation,
residential tenancies in Dubai are regulated locally by Law No. 26 of
2007, enforced by the Real Estate Regulatory Agency (RERA), a branch of
the Dubai Land Department. Every lease must be registered in the Ejari
system — an unregistered contract generally cannot be relied on before
Dubai's judicial or government authorities, including to renew a
utility connection or resolve a dispute. Rent increases on renewal are
capped by a tiered formula tied to the RERA Rental Index (introduced by
Decree No. 43 of 2013), which compares the current rent to the average
market rent for similar units and limits how much an increase can be.

Ending a tenancy is tightly restricted while the underlying contract
regime remains close to automatic renewal: a landlord who wants to
retake the property for personal use, sale, or renovation must serve
written notice — via notary public or registered mail — at least 12
months before the renewal date, even though the lease itself may run
year to year. Shorter, ground-specific notice (around 90 days) applies
to other permitted reasons such as non-payment.

Tenancy disputes in Dubai — over rent increases, deposit deductions, or
eviction notices — are heard by the Rental Dispute Settlement Centre
(RDSC), a specialized tribunal under the Dubai Land Department, rather
than the ordinary civil courts.

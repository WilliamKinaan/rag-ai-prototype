# Setting Up a Company in the Netherlands — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._

The most common vehicle for founders in the Netherlands is the BV
(besloten vennootschap), a private limited liability company. Unlike some
neighboring systems, forming a BV cannot be done by private agreement
alone: it legally requires a notarial deed of incorporation (notariële
akte), executed before a civil-law notary (notaris), who verifies the
articles of association (statuten) and formally establishes the company.
There is generally no legally mandated minimum share capital, following a
reform that removed the older fixed-capital requirement — founders can
set the capital themselves, even a nominal amount.

After the notarial deed is signed, the company must be registered in the
trade register kept by the Dutch Chamber of Commerce (KVK — Kamer van
Koophandel), which issues a registration number used in official filings
and correspondence. The company's managing director(s) (bestuurder(s))
are also recorded in this register, along with the registered business
address.

Because the notary plays a central, mandatory role, founders in the
Netherlands typically work with one throughout the incorporation process
rather than only in exceptional cases.

# UAE Marriage and Divorce Law for Non-Muslims — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from Federal Decree-Law No. 41 of 2022 on Civil Personal Status
(in force 1 Feb 2023), as summarized on the UAE Government's official
platform (u.ae)._

Federal Decree-Law No. 41 of 2022 created a separate civil personal
status track specifically for non-Muslims in the UAE, running alongside
the Sharia-based personal status rules that continue to apply to
Muslims by default. Under this civil track, a couple can marry before
an authentication judge without needing to satisfy Sharia-based
conditions, provided both parties are at least 21 years old and meet
the law's other formal requirements — a notably different threshold
from Sharia-based marriage rules, which do not set the same minimum age.

Divorce under the civil track is markedly more permissive than the
fault- or mediation-based process many expats might expect: either
spouse can obtain a no-fault divorce unilaterally through the courts,
without needing to prove wrongdoing by the other party or persuade a
judge that the marriage has broken down for a specific reason. The law
also states an intent toward equal treatment of men and women in related
matters — testimony, inheritance shares, the right to initiate divorce,
and joint custody arrangements after divorce — areas where the
Sharia-based default track can differ.

Non-Muslim residents are not automatically bound by this civil track:
the law allows them to instead choose to apply their home country's law
to their marriage, divorce, or inheritance matters, or another personal
status regime available in the UAE, rather than defaulting to either
Sharia rules or the new civil track.

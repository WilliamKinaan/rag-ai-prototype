# UAE Media Regulation — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from Federal Decree-Law No. 55 of 2023 Regulating Media (in
force 18 Dec 2024) and its executive regulation, UAE Media Council
official materials (uaemc.gov.ae)._

Federal Decree-Law No. 55 of 2023 regulates "media activity" broadly
defined — producing, circulating, printing, or publishing media content
— which explicitly reaches well beyond traditional broadcasters and
newspapers to cover digital platforms and, notably, social media
influencers, who are treated as a licensable media activity in their own
right rather than left outside the framework the way many older press
laws would leave them.

Licensing sits with the UAE Media Council, which issues permits across
print, audio, visual, broadcasting, and digital media activities — an
unlicensed influencer or content creator engaging in activity the law
treats as "media" is operating outside the framework, not just in a gray
area. Beyond licensing, the law sets content standards meant to align
published material with UAE societal values, restricts certain kinds of
reporting, addresses defamation, and includes measures specifically
aimed at combating misinformation — a more explicit anti-misinformation
mandate than most traditional press laws carried.

The law also extends into free zones dedicated to media, meaning a media
business operating out of a UAE media free zone doesn't automatically
sit outside this framework the way some other regulatory regimes treat
free zone activity as separate from federal law.

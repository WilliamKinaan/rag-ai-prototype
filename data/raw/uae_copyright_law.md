# UAE Copyright Law — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from Federal Decree-Law No. 38 of 2021 on Copyright and
Neighboring Rights (in force 2 Jan 2022) and its executive regulation
(Cabinet Resolution No. 47 of 2022), Ministry of Economy & Tourism
official materials._

Federal Decree-Law No. 38 of 2021 protects original literary, artistic,
and creative works — including software, databases, and digital content
— giving authors both economic rights (to exploit and license the work
commercially) and moral rights (such as attribution) that are generally
treated as belonging to the author regardless of who ends up owning the
economic rights. Unlike trademarks, copyright protection arises
automatically on creation of an original work rather than requiring
registration, though the law does provide an optional registration
system with the Ministry of Economy that can help evidence ownership in
a dispute.

The 2021 update specifically targeted the digital economy, updating
provisions for online distribution, collective rights management, and
mandatory licensing procedures, and strengthening enforcement tools
against digital piracy — reflecting how much content consumption had
shifted online since the prior copyright law. Both civil remedies
(injunctions, damages) and criminal penalties are available against
infringers, with the criminal route generally reserved for commercial-
scale or repeat infringement rather than incidental personal use.

Because copyright doesn't require registration to exist, disputes often
turn on evidence of authorship and creation date — which is part of why
the optional Ministry of Economy registration, timestamps, and similar
documentation are commonly recommended even though they're not legally
mandatory.

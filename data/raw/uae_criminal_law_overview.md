# UAE Criminal Law — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from Federal Decree-Law No. 31 of 2021 on Crimes and Penalties
(the Penal Code, in force 2 Jan 2022, replacing the 1987 Penal Code),
as summarized by official and reputable legal-industry sources._

Federal Decree-Law No. 31 of 2021 is the UAE's general criminal code,
setting out principles of criminal liability, classifying offences
(against persons, property, public order, and public safety, among
others), and prescribing penalties ranging from fines through
imprisonment to, for the most serious crimes, capital punishment. The
lightest tier, an "infraction," can carry as little as 24 hours and as
much as 10 days of custody, illustrating how broadly the code's
penalty ladder is graded rather than treating all offences alike.

Compared to many Western systems, the UAE's criminal law reflects a mix
of civil-law codification and Islamic-law-influenced provisions,
particularly around morality-related offences, and applies to residents
and visitors alike regardless of nationality — there is no separate,
lighter regime for foreigners. The 2021 update also modernized the code
to explicitly address newer categories of harm such as cybercrime,
human-trafficking-adjacent offences, and drug-related crimes, working
alongside dedicated standalone laws on those specific topics rather than
replacing them.

Criminal cases proceed through public prosecution (the Public
Prosecution investigates and decides whether to charge) rather than
private prosecution by the victim, and then through the ordinary Court
of First Instance, Court of Appeal, and Court of Cassation structure
that also hears civil matters.

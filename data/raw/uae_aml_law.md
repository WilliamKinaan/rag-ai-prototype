# UAE Anti-Money Laundering Law — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from Federal Decree-Law No. 20 of 2018 on Anti-Money Laundering
and Combating the Financing of Terrorism (as amended by Federal
Decree-Law No. 7 of 2024), UAE Ministry of Justice and Central Bank
official materials._

Federal Decree-Law No. 20 of 2018 is the UAE's central AML/CFT statute,
replacing an earlier 2002 law and imposing obligations on banks,
exchange houses, real estate agents, dealers in precious metals and
stones, and other "designated non-financial businesses and professions"
to identify customers, monitor transactions, and report suspicious
activity to the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU). A defining feature of
the law is that money laundering is treated as an independent offence:
someone can be prosecuted and punished for laundering proceeds even if
they were never charged with — or were acquitted of — the underlying
crime that generated the money.

The 2024 amendment (Federal Decree-Law No. 7 of 2024) established a
National Committee to Combat Money Laundering, Financing of Terrorism,
and Financing of Illegal Organisations, reflecting continued regulatory
tightening after the UAE's 2022 placement on the Financial Action Task
Force (FATF) "grey list," from which it was later removed following
reforms including strengthened enforcement under this law.

Executive Regulations (Cabinet Resolution No. 10 of 2019, as updated)
set out the detailed customer due-diligence and reporting procedures
regulated entities must follow, and penalties for non-compliance can
include significant fines against both the institution and responsible
individuals, separate from any criminal liability for the underlying
laundering offence itself.

# UAE Involuntary Loss of Employment Insurance (ILOE) — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from Federal Decree-Law No. 13 of 2022 and official ILOE scheme
materials (iloe.ae), launched January 2023 by MOHRE._

The ILOE scheme is a mandatory unemployment insurance requirement for
almost all employees in the UAE — both private-sector and federal
government workers — rather than an optional product an employee might
choose to buy. Workers pay a modest monthly premium themselves (not the
employer), and in return become eligible for temporary monthly cash
compensation, up to 60% of their average basic salary over the prior six
months, for a maximum of three consecutive months if they lose their job
involuntarily.

Eligibility is narrower than it might first sound: a claimant must have
been subscribed for at least 12 consecutive months before the job loss,
the job loss must not be due to resignation or a disciplinary dismissal,
the claimant must still be a UAE resident and not flagged as an
absconding worker, and the claim must be filed within 30 days of the
termination date. Missing the subscription requirement or the payment
obligation carries its own fines (distinct from, and in addition to, not
receiving the benefit) — a fixed fine for failing to subscribe at all,
and a smaller fine for failing to keep up with premium payments.

Administration sits outside MOHRE's usual complaint-and-labour-court
process entirely: subscription and claims are handled through the
dedicated ILOE Insurance Pool (represented by Dubai Insurance company)
via the scheme's own portal, not through the standard MOHRE labour
dispute channel used for wage or dismissal complaints.

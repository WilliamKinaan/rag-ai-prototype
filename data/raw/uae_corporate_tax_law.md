# UAE Corporate Tax Law — Overview

_Illustrative simplified summary for a search demo — not legal advice._
_Sourced from Federal Decree-Law No. 47 of 2022 on the Taxation of
Corporations and Businesses, as published by the Ministry of Finance
and Federal Tax Authority (tax.gov.ae)._

Federal Decree-Law No. 47 of 2022 introduced the UAE's first-ever
federal corporate tax, applying to financial years starting on or after
1 June 2023 — a significant shift for a country long marketed
internationally as a no-corporate-tax jurisdiction. The standard rate is
9% on taxable income, but only on income above AED 375,000; income at or
below that threshold is taxed at 0%, a design specifically meant to
shield small businesses and startups from the new tax rather than
applying a flat rate to every company regardless of size.

Free zone companies are not automatically exempt: a "Qualifying Free
Zone Person" can still benefit from a 0% rate, but only on "qualifying
income" that meets specific substance and activity conditions set by
the Ministry of Finance and Cabinet decisions — income outside those
conditions, or earned from mainland UAE activity, can still be taxed at
the standard rate. This means a free zone licence alone no longer
guarantees a company pays no corporate tax, unlike the position before
this law existed.

Businesses must register with the Federal Tax Authority for corporate
tax purposes and file an annual return, generally within nine months of
the end of their financial year, separately from any VAT registration
and filing obligations they may also have.
